== 0. ONE-PAGE STATEMENT: WHAT PARTICIPATION DATA THIS PROJECT HAS ==

1. THERE IS NO ROUTES-RUN COLUMN ANYWHERE IN FREE NFLVERSE DATA. `route` in
   load_participation is ONE label per play. In 2022 it describes the TARGETED
   receiver's route and nothing else: present on 99.4% of targeted pass
   plays, on 0.0% of untargeted pass plays, on 0 non-pass plays, 0 multi-label
   rows. In 2024 it is still one label per play (98.7% of targeted plays)
   but ALSO appears on 32.5% of UNtargeted pass plays (1489 plays —
   intended-route charting on throwaways/sacks). Per-player route counts cannot
   be built from it in any season.

2. THE FILE IS TWO DATA PRODUCTS UNDER ONE NAME (S8), breaking at 2023:
   - 2016-2022: offense_players non-empty on 91.3-91.6% of plays — but the
     empty rows are NOT missing scrimmage data: classified for 2022, all
     4292 are {'NOT_IN_PBP': 2158, 'no_play': 2134}
     (no_play = pre-snap penalties; NOT_IN_PBP = timeouts/admin rows).
     On REG dropbacks coverage is 100.0% in every season (2016: 1 play short).
     Absent route is NaN (so .notna() and the honest test agree, ~36-39%).
   - 2023-2025: offense_players 100.0%; absent route is EMPTY STRING, so
     .notna() reports 100.0% while the honest rate is 41.6-42.5%. This is the
     prior scoping's '100% coverage' artifact. ALWAYS .fillna('').str.len()>0.
   - The route VOCABULARY also changed (HITCH/OUT/CROSS/IN/FLAT/ANGLE ->
     HITCH/CURL, QUICK OUT, IN/DIG, DEEP OUT, SHALLOW CROSS/DRAG, SWING,
     TEXAS/ANGLE). Never pool route labels across the 2022/2023 boundary.

3. THE FREE CEILING IS PASS-SNAP PARTICIPATION — share of team dropbacks on
   which a gsis_id appears in offense_players. Built here for 2016-2025 (10
   seasons, 93045 player-week rows -> pass_snap_participation.parquet),
   REG only. Denominator = dropbacks with non-empty offense_players, which is
   measured to be a no-op safeguard (100.0% covered every season) — the ~9%
   overall empty rate pre-2023 lives entirely in no_plays/admin rows.

4. THE BLOCKER CONFLATION IS REAL BUT SMALLER THAN THE PRIOR SCOPING ASSUMED,
   because coaches BENCH the blocking archetype on dropbacks. Classic blocking
   TEs sit at 23%-29% pass-snap share (vs 80%-98% for route runners), so the
   metric already separates the archetypes. The residual conflation: even at a
   conservative per-route target-earn floor (half the route-runner minimum,
   f=0.122/route), pass-snap share overstates the implied route-share upper
   bound by: Luke Farrell >=5%, Charlie Woerner >=6%, Gus Edwards >=17%. Population-wide, bottom-
   quartile TE target-earners still log 49% mean pass-snap share vs 75% for
   top-quartile — a TE at ~50% share is ambiguous between a part-time route
   runner and a full-time player who blocks on half his dropbacks. Every
   conclusion built on pass-snap participation must carry this bound; it is
   snap share restricted to dropbacks, NOT a route signal (charter 3.3.10).

5. VALIDATION vs load_snap_counts: route runners' pass-snap share runs ABOVE
   their overall offense_pct (+2.5% to +12.8% — they stay on for passing
   downs); blockers/early-down RBs run BELOW (-18.9% to -8.1%). Both
   directions are the expected physics, and no share left [0,1].

== 3. PASS-SNAP PARTICIPATION BUILD ==
   definition: for each (season, week, gsis_id, team):
     snaps_on_dropbacks = # REG-season plays with qb_dropback==1 where the id
       appears in offense_players (joined pbp<->participation on
       (nflverse_game_id, play_id); offense_players split on ';')
     team_dropbacks = that team-week's dropbacks WITH non-empty offense_players.
       Measured: an inert safeguard — dropback coverage is 100.0% in every
       season (2016 is 1 play short); the pre-2023 ~9% empty rows are
       no_plays and non-pbp admin rows, not scrimmage plays.
     pass_snap_share = snaps_on_dropbacks / team_dropbacks
   output: pass_snap_participation.parquet · 93045 rows
