D. REPLICATION TEST — does 'capital matters only when there's no role' hold everywhere?
====================================================================================
  Found in R2-3 (+42.3pp among no-role players vs +0.6pp among those with a role, n=38).
  A result that big on that sample is either a real mechanism or a fluke. Replication in
  bands it was NOT found in is how we tell. Nothing below is tuned.

  --- NFL draft capital top-32 ---
  band      NO role: lift      P     n     HAS role: lift      P     n
  R1            (too few)           12              +8.9   0.83   133
  R2-3             +42.3   1.00    38              +0.6   0.53   205
  R4-6             -14.0   0.17    88              -6.8   0.15   255
  R7-10            +12.5   0.93   194              +3.1   0.68   288

  --- NFL draft capital top-64 ---
  band      NO role: lift      P     n     HAS role: lift      P     n
  R1            (too few)           12              +6.2   0.75   133
  R2-3             +33.9   0.99    38              +5.6   0.77   205
  R4-6              +0.8   0.52    88              -2.1   0.37   255
  R7-10             +0.5   0.52   194              -0.3   0.49   288

  READ: the rule replicates if the NO-role column is consistently large and positive while
  the HAS-role column sits near zero, across bands. Judge it on the pattern, not one cell.

  VERDICT — IT DOES NOT REPLICATE.
  top-32 NO-role goes +42.3 (R2-3, n=38) -> -14.0 (R4-6, n=88) -> +12.5 (R7-10, n=194):
  it REVERSES in the band with more than twice the sample it was discovered in. And the
  top-64 version — the same rule one notch wider — collapses to +0.8 and +0.5 outside
  R2-3. A real mechanism does not evaporate when the cutoff moves from 32 to 64.
  CONCLUSION: the +42.3pp was almost certainly a fluke of a 38-season cell. WITHDRAWN.

  WHAT SURVIVES: the OTHER half. Capital is near-inert for a player who already HAS a
  role — +8.9 / +0.6 / -6.8 / +3.1 across the four bands, never meaningful. So 'do not
  fade an established-role player for a late draft slot' still stands; 'trust a no-role
  player because of pedigree' does not.
